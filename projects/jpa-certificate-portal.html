Overview

The JPA Certificate Portal is the admin system behind the Jordanian Pharmacists Association's continuing-education program. Every training course the association runs — sponsored by pharmaceutical companies, delivered by collaborators, or hosted at a conference — is created here, its attendees are tracked here, and its certificates are issued, emailed, and synchronized with Oracle from here.

At the time of writing the portal manages 12,660 users, 609 courses, and 116,894 issued certificates. Courses are classified as sponsor courses (205) or collaborator courses (13), attendance is recorded with real in/out timestamps, and certificates are generated only for attendees who qualify. The whole pipeline is auditable: every certificate carries its survey, mailed, and Oracle status, and failures are surfaced instead of disappearing.

  • Course management with course type, place, sponsor, and start/end dates.
  • Lecturer, user, and attendance records tied to each course.
  • Automatic attendance-hour calculation from in and out timestamps.
  • Certificate generation with bulk actions: regenerate, resend, remove unsent, export.
  • Email delivery through SMTP and Mailgun, with per-channel statistics.
  • Oracle integration to send and verify certificates against the association's system.
  • Excel export and filters across all, sponsor, and collaborator courses.

My role

I built the portal end to end — the data model, the admin interfaces, the certificate pipeline, the email delivery, and the Oracle integration.

  • Designed the schema for courses, lecturers, users, attendance, certificates, and sponsors.
  • Built the admin dashboard with live KPIs, course-type filters, and Excel export.
  • Implemented the training calendar and per-course attendance sheets with computed attend hours.
  • Built the certificate pipeline: generation, PDF output, bulk regenerate, and email resend.
  • Integrated Oracle to push certificates and verify which ones were accepted, with retry for failures.

  • Built the supporting admin tools: lecturers, course types, places, settings, hours sheets, technical support, and logs.

Key features

Executive dashboard

Users, certificates issued, total courses, open courses, and the sponsor/collaborator split — with one-click Excel export.

Training calendar

A month view of scheduled courses — regulatory affairs, medical auditing, antimicrobial resistance, and conferences — each with its course ID.

Attendance tracking

Per-user in and out timestamps with automatically computed attendance hours, so eligibility is based on real participation.

Certificate pipeline

Generate, regenerate, resend, or remove certificates in bulk, with survey, mailed, and Oracle status visible per row.

Email delivery

SMTP and Mailgun sending with counters for successful sends — 37,100 Paratune emails recorded after the stats migration.

Oracle synchronization

Send certificates to Oracle and verify them afterwards, so the association's record and the portal never drift apart.

Tech stack

An internal admin portal, so reliability matters more than spectacle: every action that touches certificates has to be repeatable, traceable, and safe to retry.

  • Laravel
  • PHP
  • MySQL
  • REST API
  • Oracle integration
  • SMTP & Mailgun
  • Excel export
  • Role-based access control
  • Git

Architecture: a Laravel admin application over a MySQL schema built around courses, attendance, and certificates, with an Oracle adapter that pushes and verifies certificate records, and an email layer that tracks every send across two providers so nothing is silently lost.

Outcome

The portal turned a manual certification process into a pipeline: 609 courses managed, 116,894 certificates generated, 82,109 legacy certificates migrated, and 12,693 unique attendees tracked — all from one dashboard with live totals and Excel exports.

Because every certificate shows its survey, mailing, and Oracle status, the team can see exactly what was delivered and what needs attention. At the time of capture, 2,112 failed certificates were visible and retryable rather than buried in a log file.
